CHANGING THE STORY: FROM TROUBLESOME WASTE TO REAL VALUE.

We turn problem waste into real value. We are starting with water hyacinth and market organic waste, and will take on other waste routes as we grow, creating cleaner waterways, clean cooking energy, rich farm fertilizer, and stronger Niger Delta communities.

For years, problem waste, from invasive water hyacinth to market organic waste, has been treated as an endless headache that drains time and money with nothing to show afterward. The Riparian Circular Solutions Limited changes that story, building a local, community-centered Delta State enterprise rooted in circularity by design and environmental literacy.

Starting in Aladja and Pessu Market, Warri, we pair free school and community education with practical waste-to-value systems, turning what slows rivers and piles up in markets into useful products, cleaner creeks, and public Water, Sanitation & Hygiene (WASH: clean water, safe sanitation, and everyday hygiene).

The biggest surprise: 12 of 21 residents named poor waste management and open dumping as the single most urgent environmental problem in Aladja. Water hyacinth came second among those who named a single problem, with 3 residents. That is why our model treats waste and waterways together.

What We Do • Circularity by Design

Our Five-Pillar Circular Economy Model

Instead of treating invasive aquatic weeds, market food scraps, and poor sanitation as separate headaches, we link them into one self-reinforcing local value chain. Where we are now: school education and community mapping are underway. Biodigesters and WASH facilities are planned pilots.

Pillar 01

Waterway Restoration

Community-led clearing of blocked waterways, starting with invasive water hyacinth, to reopen boat corridors, restore sunlight and dissolved oxygen, and protect native Niger Delta fish habitats.

Pillar 02

Waste-to-Energy

Blending harvested water hyacinth and organic market waste inside sealed biodigesters, with more waste routes to follow, to produce clean, affordable cooking biogas for market vendors and people working on the site.

Pillar 03

Organic Fertilizer

Converting nutrient-rich biodigester slurry (digestate) into organic fertilizer for local farmers and nurseries while keeping organic waste out of drains.

Pillar 04

Environmental Education

Providing 100% free climate change, circular economy, Water, Sanitation & Hygiene (WASH), and UN SDG workshops to schools, youth clubs, and communities across Southern Nigeria.

Pillar 05

Community Water, Sanitation & Hygiene (WASH)

Planning to reinvest circular bioenergy revenues into practical Water, Sanitation & Hygiene (WASH) facilities, so busy markets, riverine settlements, and student hubs have dignified, well-maintained sanitation instead of untreated waste entering local creeks:

Water

Clean wash-water points and cleaner surrounding creeks free from sewage runoff.

Sanitation

Safe, biodigester-linked public toilets that safely treat human and organic waste.

Hygiene

Soap-and-water handwashing stations paired with everyday school and market hygiene habits.

Our Business Case

Why This Model Is Commercially & Socially Sustainable

Many river cleanups stall because they depend on recurring charity and bring in little income of their own. The Riparian Circular Solutions Limited is building a circular enterprise where environmental cleanup earns its own income and abundant, locally available feedstocks become useful local products.

1. Waterway Clearance Contracts

Paid waterway clearance for government agencies and companies that depend on open waterways, priced per maintained stretch or on a monthly retainer, alongside waste collection fees and ESG or climate-finance grants tied to verified methane abatement.

2. Biogas for Market Vendors & Site Users

Recurring sales of affordable cooking biogas to market vendors and people working on the site, starting at Pessu Market and replacing costly LPG and firewood.

3. Organic Bio-Fertilizer Sales

Commercializing nutrient-rich digestate slurry to Delta State smallholder farmers, fish-pond operators, and nurseries seeking affordable organic soil inputs.

4. Funding Public WASH & Youth Education

As revenue grows, operating surpluses are meant to help fund public Water, Sanitation & Hygiene (WASH) facilities: clean toilets and handwashing stations, while keeping our school climate workshops 100% free.

How these estimates are calculated
  • Waterway cleared: about 40 m² per tonne of fresh hyacinth, assuming a mat density of roughly 25 kg/m². Dense mats can hold more, which would mean less area per tonne.
  • Biogas: about 15 m³ per tonne of fresh hyacinth (it is over 90% water) and about 60 m³ per tonne of market food waste. Real yields vary widely with feedstock, temperature, and how well the digester is run.
  • Liquid bio-fertilizer: nearly all input mass leaves as wet digestate (about 0.95 t per tonne fed in, before any added water). Dried or solid fertilizer would weigh much less.
  • CO₂e avoided: about 3 kg CO₂e per m³ of biogas used instead of firewood or charcoal, plus about 0.4 t CO₂e per tonne of market waste kept from rotting in dumps and drains. No methane credit is claimed for the hyacinth. These are not verified carbon credits.

What's the Science Behind This?

While our main website and school workshops use straightforward everyday language, the circular system we are developing rests on established principles of aquatic limnology, anaerobic microbiology, biochemical engineering, agronomy, and environmental health.

01 • Aquatic Ecology & Limnology

Eutrophication & Dissolved Oxygen (DO)

Pontederia crassipes (water hyacinth) exhibits rapid clonal proliferation under nutrient-enriched (eutrophic) conditions driven by excess nitrogen (NO3− / NH4+) and phosphorus (PO43−) runoff. Dense floating mats attenuate photosynthetically active radiation (PAR), suppress submerged aquatic photosynthesis, restrict wind-driven gas exchange, and elevate biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) as senescent plant tissue decomposes, driving hypoxia that stresses native fish and benthic organisms.

02 • Anaerobic Microbiology

Four-Stage Anaerobic Co-Digestion

In a sealed, oxygen-free reactor (oxidation-reduction potential roughly −200 to −400 mV during methanogenesis), microbial communities carry out hydrolysis (breaking cellulose, hemicellulose, and proteins into sugars and amino acids), acidogenesis (forming volatile fatty acids such as propionate and butyrate), acetogenesis (converting these into acetate, H2, and CO2), and methanogenesis (acetoclastic and hydrogenotrophic archaea producing CH4). Hyacinth's lignin-rich fiber slows hydrolysis, so chopping or pre-treatment helps. Co-digesting it with easily degradable market food waste improves buffering and process stability, and the feedstock blend is adjusted to keep the Carbon-to-Nitrogen (C:N) ratio near the commonly cited 20–30:1 range.

03 • Bioenergy & Atmospheric Chemistry

Biomethane Yield & Methane Abatement

Raw biogas is mainly methane (CH4, typically about 50–70%) and carbon dioxide (CO2, about 30–50%), with traces of hydrogen sulfide (H2S) and water vapor. Methane traps far more heat than CO2: roughly 27 times more per kilogram over 100 years and about 80 times more over 20 years (IPCC AR6 values for non-fossil methane). Capturing it in a well-sealed digester and burning it for cooking, rather than letting waste rot uncontrolled, turns it into CO2 and reduces climate impact. Using biogas instead of firewood or charcoal also cuts household smoke. These benefits depend on keeping leaks low, because escaped methane quickly erodes the gain.

04 • Soil Biogeochemistry & Agronomy

Nutrients & Safety in Digested Slurry

During anaerobic digestion, much of the organic nitrogen is converted to plant-available ammonium (NH4+), while phosphorus and potassium stay in the slurry. Applied at the right rates, digestate returns organic matter and nutrients to soil and can reduce reliance on synthetic fertilizer. Ammonia can escape to the air if slurry is left uncovered or surface-spread, and over-application can wash nutrients back into waterways, so storage and dosing matter. Digestion at warm (mesophilic) temperatures reduces pathogens but does not eliminate them. Digestate from animal by-products, such as slaughter waste, needs further treatment (for example, pasteurization or thorough composting) before use on food crops.

05 • Environmental & Public Health

WASH Barriers & Fecal-Oral Pathway Control

Water, Sanitation & Hygiene (WASH: clean water, safe sanitation, and everyday hygiene) interrupts enteric pathogen transmission along fecal-oral routes. Engineered sanitation containment, clean water access, and soap-assisted handwashing lower microbial loads in high-footfall spaces, such as markets, riverine settlements, and schools, and reduce coliform and nutrient contamination entering surface waters.

06 • Process Control & Verification

Hydraulic Retention Time & Reactor Stability

Sustained digester performance depends on maintaining Hydraulic Retention Time (HRT) long enough for slow-growing methanogens to reproduce without washout, keeping reactor temperature within a stable mesophilic range, and monitoring pH and volatile fatty acid accumulation so acidogenesis does not inhibit methanogenesis.
